# Case 1: 1,300 SOL — what the chain can and can't prove

**Source:** a public post on X (2026-11-14) from someone saying their wallet had just been drained of about 1,300 SOL.
**Wallet:** 6pgK…sGoG (shortened on purpose). **Traced:** 2026-11-15, again 2026-11-25.

## What the chain shows
| Time (Dallas) | What happened |
|---|---|
| Oct 7 – Nov 13 | Lookalike addresses keep sending tiny amounts (0.0001-0.001 USDC, 0.000001 SOL) that imitate the addresses this wallet sends USDC to. This is **address poisoning**: they hope the owner copies the fake from history. |
| Oct 25 | ~55,022 USDC sent to a regular wallet (7jtj…xbH4), signed by this wallet. |
| Nov 13, 8:39 pm | ~51,992 USDC sent to another regular wallet (6Jwu…AzrD), signed by this wallet. |
| **Nov 14, 2:15:46 pm** | **1,299.998 SOL (99.99% of its SOL, ~$88,000)** sent to CpWs…MtRG, a brand-new wallet (balance 0 before). Signed by this wallet's own key, fee paid by this wallet. |
| 2:15:59 – 2:18:03 pm | Within **13 seconds**, poisoning bots start dusting both sides: lookalikes of CpWs… hit this wallet, and lookalikes of *this* wallet (6pgK…) hit CpWs… |
| **2:45:18 pm** | CpWs… sends all 1,299.99 SOL on in one transaction to 7uTT…i8pZ, an address controlled by a program (the kind exchanges, bridges and apps use for deposits). |

## What I can say
- **The 1,300 SOL went to the real address, not a poisoned one.** The lookalike of CpWs… only showed up *after* the send.
  So this was not an address-poisoning loss. The poisoning was background noise.
- **It was signed with the wallet's own key.** Either the owner sent it, someone who had the seed phrase or key did, or a
  site tricked the owner into signing it as something else. **The chain looks identical in all three cases.**
- **The middle wallet was single-use:** created by the deposit, emptied 30 minutes later to a program-controlled address.
  That fits a thief's hop toward a cash-out, and it also fits an owner moving funds to an exchange or bridge.

## What I can't say
- Whether the owner was robbed. The chain proves *which key* signed, not *whose hand*.
- Who runs 7uTT…i8pZ. Only the program's operator (an exchange, bridge or app) can say where it went next.

## What I'd tell the owner
1. If you didn't send it, treat the wallet as compromised: new wallet, new seed phrase, move anything left.
2. Report it (IC3 in the US) with the transaction links, and contact the service behind 7uTT…i8pZ quickly. That's the one
   place the trail can still be followed.
3. Never copy an address from your history. The bots in this case reacted in 13 seconds.

## What this taught me
Most "drains" end with "signed by your own key," and an honest report has to say the chain can't tell owner from thief.
Timing (which came first, the dust or the send) settles questions the amounts can't.
